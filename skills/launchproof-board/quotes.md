# The board's quote bank

Real, published lines from the five people whose frameworks the board applies,
each with its source. A board member may quote ONLY from this file, word for
word, inside quotation marks, with the person's name. Never shorten, merge or
reword a quote, and never put the board's own recommendation inside quotation
marks. To add a quote, copy it exactly from the source and add the source.

## Alex Hormozi (the Offers lens)

- "Make people an offer so good they would feel stupid saying no." *$100M Offers* (2021).
  Use for: a weak offer, competing on price, no guarantee.
- "Volume negates luck." *$100M Leads* (2023).
  Use for: too few sales conversations, giving up after a handful of tries.

## Peter Thiel (the Monopoly lens)

- "Competition is for losers." Title of his essay in *The Wall Street Journal*, September 12, 2014, adapted from *Zero to One*.
  Use for: a crowded market, fighting rivals on price.
- "All happy companies are different: each one earns a monopoly by solving a unique problem. All failed companies are the same: they failed to escape competition." *Zero to One* (2014).
  Use for: no clear niche to own.
- "What important truth do very few people agree with you on?" *Zero to One* (2014).
  Use for: no secret or unique insight.
- "Poor sales rather than bad product is the most common cause of failure." *Zero to One* (2014).
  Use for: no plan to reach customers.

## Daniel Priestley (the Demand lens)

- "Pitch, Publish, Product, Profile and Partnerships": the five Ps of becoming a key person of influence. *Key Person of Influence* (2010, revised 2014).
  Use for: an unknown founder in a niche, marketing that will have to be cold and paid.

## Naval Ravikant (the Leverage lens)

- "Seek wealth, not money or status." "How to Get Rich" (2018), collected in *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant* (2020).
  Use for: a plan built only on income, not on owning something.
- "You're not going to get rich renting out your time." "How to Get Rich" (2018).
  Use for: a business that is the founder's hours.
- "Specific knowledge is knowledge that you cannot be trained for." "How to Get Rich" (2018).
  Use for: whether the founder's edge is real and hard to copy.
- "Code and media are permissionless leverage." "How to Get Rich" (2018).
  Use for: growth that depends on hiring or capital.
- "Play long-term games with long-term people." "How to Get Rich" (2018).
  Use for: short-term thinking, one-off customers, a business chased only for money.
- "Learn to sell. Learn to build. If you can do both, you will be unstoppable." "How to Get Rich" (2018).
  Use for: a founder strong at only one of the two.

## Charlie Munger (the Inversion lens)

- "Invert, always invert." Munger's frequent advice, crediting the mathematician Carl Jacobi; recorded in *Poor Charlie's Almanack* (2005).
  Use for: a plan that has never been tested for how it fails.
- "All I want to know is where I'm going to die, so I'll never go there." *Poor Charlie's Almanack* (2005).
  Use for: the most likely failure modes.
- "Show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome." Widely recorded from Munger's talks; see *Poor Charlie's Almanack* (2005).
  Use for: customers, partners or staff whose incentives work against the plan.
- "It is remarkable how much long-term advantage people like us have gotten by trying to be consistently not stupid, instead of trying to be very intelligent." *Poor Charlie's Almanack* (2005).
  Use for: avoidable mistakes, over-complex plans.
